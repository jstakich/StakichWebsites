How Much Does a Website Cost for a Small Business? (2026 Prices)

By Jeremy Stakich · October 9, 2026 · PricingSmall business

Quick answer: A small business website usually costs $500 to $3,000 to build with a freelancer or small studio, plus $30 to $150 per month for hosting, security, and updates. DIY options like GoDaddy, Bluehost, and Wix look cheaper, but you build and maintain the site yourself and pay yearly add-on fees. Agencies often charge $5,000 or more.

What you're actually paying for

  • Design and setup: the layout, words, photos, and pages for your services.
  • Domain name: your web address, about $10–$20 per year.
  • Hosting and security: keeping the site online, fast, and protected with HTTPS.
  • Updates: changing prices, hours, photos, and services as your business grows.
  • Google setup: page titles, local search info, and speed so customers can find you.

How much does a website cost per month for a small business?

Most small businesses pay $30 to $150 per month to keep a professionally built site running. The low end covers hosting, security, and small edits. The higher end usually adds blog posts and social media content, which bring in new visitors over time.

How much does it cost to maintain a website?

If you build it yourself, maintenance is "free" but costs you hours: plugin updates, broken pages, and security fixes. Paying someone $30–$80 a month to handle it is usually cheaper than an hour of your own time each week.

What about Bluehost and GoDaddy?

With Bluehost and GoDaddy, you build and maintain the website yourself. They rent you the tools, but you do the design, the writing, the photos, and the Google setup. After launch, you also keep it running: software updates, security fixes, broken pages, backups, and every change to your hours, prices, or photos. They also advertise very low prices, but the price on the ad is rarely the price you keep paying.

Bluehost: the downsides

  • The $3.99 price needs a 3-year prepay. You pay about $144 up front, and it renews at $9.99 to $15.99 a month.
  • It's only hosting. You build the site yourself in WordPress, then maintain it: plugin updates, security fixes, and anything that breaks.
  • The free domain is free for 1 year only. After that you pay for it every year, and Bluehost charges a fee to keep the domain if you cancel.
  • No phone support on the starter plan. Help is chat only.
  • Add-ons at checkout. Extra security and backup tools are offered that can raise the bill.

GoDaddy: the downsides

  • Prices jump after year 1. The website builder starts at $9.99 a month and auto-renews at about $15 a month. Basic hosting starts at $5.99 on a 3-year term and renews at $11.99.
  • The free plan can't use your own domain. Your site lives on a GoDaddy address until you upgrade.
  • Hard to move later. Booking, forms, and email are tied to GoDaddy's tools, so switching usually means rebuilding.
  • You still do the work. Building it, writing, photos, Google setup, and maintaining it after launch are all on you.

The yearly fees they don't put in the ad

On top of the monthly plan, both companies charge yearly fees for things most businesses need. These are offered at checkout and renew automatically every year. GoDaddy's first-year domain is a penny, but you have to buy 3 years at once.

The bottom line: Bluehost and GoDaddy are cheap tools, not a finished website. If you have the time and like tech, they can work. If you'd rather run your business, paying someone a fair, flat price is often cheaper once you count renewals and your own hours. Red flags that you're overpaying

  • You can't take your domain name with you. Make sure the designer will transfer it to you if you ever leave.
  • Long contracts (12–36 months) with big cancellation fees.
  • "Setup fees" for things that take minutes, like connecting your domain.
  • No clear list of what the monthly fee includes.

Frequently asked questions

Is $500 enough for a small business website?

Yes, for a clean 3–5 page site from a freelancer or small studio. Expect to add a monthly fee for hosting and updates.

Is Wix cheaper than hiring someone?

Upfront, yes. Over a few years, a DIY builder at $25–$50 per month plus your time often costs as much as hiring someone.

Is Bluehost or GoDaddy cheaper than hiring someone?

At first, yes. But Bluehost's low price needs a 3-year prepay and renews higher, GoDaddy's prices rise after year 1, and both add yearly fees for the domain, email, security, and backups. Many owners end up paying $300 to $450 or more a year, and they still build and maintain the site themselves.

Do I own my website if I pay monthly?

With most monthly plans, the design stays with the web company, but your domain, logo, photos, and words should go with you if you leave. Ask any web designer this before you pay.
