Website Design for Contractors: What Gets You More Jobs in 2026

By Jeremy Stakich · October 5, 2026 · ContractorsTrades

Quick answer: Good website design for contractors puts trust and photos first: licenses and insurance at the top, a gallery of finished projects, clear services, reviews, financing info, and a simple estimate form, all fast and easy to use on a phone.

Why contractors need a strong website

Jobs are big and customers are careful. Before calling, most homeowners compare 2–3 contractors online. Your website is your first impression, and often the reason you get the call instead of the next company.

Pages every contractor website needs

  • Home: who you are, where you work, and a big "Free Estimate" button.
  • Services: a section for each job type (roofing, remodels, decks, siding).
  • Project gallery: before-and-after photos with short descriptions.
  • About: your story, years in business, and team photos.
  • Reviews: real customer reviews from Google.
  • Contact: phone, estimate form, and service area.

Trust signals that win bigger jobs

  • License numbers and proof of insurance
  • Manufacturer certifications (like GAF or Owens Corning)
  • Warranties in plain language
  • Financing options for large projects
  • Insurance claim help for storm damage

Website design for construction companies vs. general contractors

Construction companies working on commercial projects should highlight capacity, safety record, and past clients. General contractors and remodelers should focus on homeowners: photos, reviews, and how easy you are to work with.

Do contractor websites need a blog?

A blog helps you show up for more searches over time, like "how much does a new roof cost."

Starting prices or typical ranges help customers trust you and reduce tire-kickers.
