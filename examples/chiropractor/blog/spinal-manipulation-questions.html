What to Know About Spinal Manipulation

By Thomas Reed · November 3, 2026

Spinal manipulation is one approach used by several types of licensed health professionals. Research summarized by the National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health suggests it may help some people with low-back pain, but results vary and the evidence is not a guarantee for any individual.

Discuss risks and alternatives

Temporary soreness, stiffness, or headache can occur after manipulation. Serious complications have been reported but are rare; discuss your individual health history, medications, and concerns with the practitioner.

Make an informed choice

  • Ask what the assessment found and what the proposed care is intended to address.
  • Discuss possible benefits, risks, and alternatives before deciding.
  • Tell the practitioner about health conditions and medications.
  • Seek care from another clinician when symptoms or your health history call for it.

This article is educational and does not replace medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. A qualified health professional can help you decide what is appropriate for you.
