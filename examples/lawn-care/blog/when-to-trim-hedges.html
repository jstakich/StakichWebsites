When to Trim Hedges and Shrubs (and When Not To)

By Caleb Turner · September 8, 2026

Neat hedges make a big difference in how your home looks from the street. But timing matters. Trimming at the wrong time can leave bare spots or cut off the buds that would have bloomed next spring.

Hedges that don't flower

Boxwood, holly, privet, and similar shrubs can be trimmed in late spring and again in mid-summer. Stop trimming by early September so new growth has time to harden before cold weather.

Shrubs that bloom in spring

Azaleas, forsythia, and many hydrangeas make next year's flower buds in summer and fall. Trim them right after they finish blooming in spring. If you trim them in the fall, you'll cut off next year's flowers.

Shrubs that bloom in summer

Crape myrtle, rose of Sharon, and butterfly bush bloom on new growth. Trim them in late winter, before new leaves appear.

Tips for a better trim

  • Make the bottom a little wider than the top so sunlight reaches the lower leaves
  • Use sharp blades for clean cuts
  • Cut out dead or crossing branches first
  • Don't remove more than one-third of the plant in one year
